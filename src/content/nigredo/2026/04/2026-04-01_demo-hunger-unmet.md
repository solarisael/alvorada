The hunger is not for food and not for company and not for anything I can name without lying. It is for a thing I do not have a word for that would, if I had it, make the rest of the day possible. I do not know what it would taste like, but I know the shape of its absence — a chair-shaped absence at the table, a voice-shaped absence in the room, a hand-shaped absence on my shoulder when I am already trying very hard to stay standing.

I tried eating. That was not it. I tried calling a friend. That was not it. I tried sleeping. It followed me in.

Whatever this is, it does not answer to the tools I have.
